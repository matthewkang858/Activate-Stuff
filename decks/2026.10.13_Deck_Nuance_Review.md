# Nuance review: "What SaaSpocalypse?" (v7)

Activate Consulting · 13 Oct 2026 · Engagement manager synthesis of five theme analyses. Every survey number below was recomputed by an independent adversarial check. This memo uses only items rated keep or keep-with-fix, and the corrected numbers replace the analysts' originals throughout.

**How to read the numbers.** Every survey figure carries its base. n < 75 is flagged as directional. A gap under ~8 pts on a base under ~200 is treated as noise. A "rating" means one company's answer for one core platform (CRM, ERP, HRM/HCM or ITSM). Pooled rating shares are not company shares, and the copy labels them that way.

**The storyline these additions build:** *Customers stay, but the screen and the AI budget move, and the movement shows up in AI maturity, not in renewal scores.*

> **Status vs. deck v7:** v7 already uses the 67% stat in place of 96%, the "prefer" title framing, and the "HOW PROVIDERS STAY STICKY" panel header on slide 10. Items below that reference "WHAT PROVIDERS NEED TO STAY STICKY", "log-ins" or the Figma pricing bullet refer to the pre-QA wording. Everything else is not yet in the deck. ## 4. Caveats to footnote

1. **Bases differ by area.** CRM, ERP and ITSM were asked only of buyers with direct experience (n = 228/141/374). HRM/HCM, Data storage, Design and Verticalized apps were asked of nearly everyone (HRM/HCM n = 578). Cross-area gaps partly reflect who answered.
2. **Ratings are not companies.** Pooled figures stack up to four platforms per company. Write "% of ratings" or give company-level numbers, and treat pooled p-values as approximate.
3. **Horizons differ.** Q26 asks about the next renewal; Q37 asks about the next three years. Don't add slide 4 and slide 6 together.
4. **"Severe or significant", never just "severe".** The switching-cost metric is the top two codes.
5. **Slide 13 routing.** The Talent gap option was shown only to the 181 respondents who said they cannot build. On the n = 601 base it understates the barrier (17% vs. 57%).
6. **Q41 format.** With up to two picks and 5 of 6 options non-native, "any non-native" (96%) is near-automatic. Shares of "chatbot choosers who also picked native" are mechanically low.
7. **"AI chatbot" includes Microsoft Copilot,** which Q70/Q71 treat as platform-embedded AI, so a chatbot can sit inside a platform.
8. **Q72 answer options differed by approach.** Platform consolidators never saw best-of-breed or vendor-dependency options. Compare reasons within groups, not across.
9. **Q37 "Build our own" means building in-house to replace the current provider.** It is not "building AI" in general.
10. **Q31–Q34 groups hold 1–5 features.** Any-in-group nets inflate bigger groups, so show the average per feature.
11. **Q36 is about all software vendors,** not the specific platform rated.
12. **Response style.** SMB respondents pick fewer options on every multi-select, so read SMB gaps on multi-selects with care. 41% of the "all four switching costs" ratings are straight-lined, though results hold without them.
13. **Cross-sectional data.** The AI-maturity and add-alongside links are associations. Avoid "erodes", "drives" and "on-ramp" on slides.
14. **Stated vs. observed.** Stated renewal intent (70%) runs below filed retention (92–98%), so treat it as conservative. Self-reported control and maturity read high, so treat those as upper bounds.
15. **External sources:**
    - SAP "restricts", not "bans".
    - Salesforce agent metering is targeted for Nov 2026 and is not live; the Flex Credit pools have no stated time period.
    - Vertice does not disclose its method.
    - ServiceNow's 98% comes from Q2 call coverage (the Q1 10-Q shows 97%); recheck it.
    - Menlo's unit changes from "solutions" (2024) to "use cases" (2025).
    - Gartner quotes come via reprints, so confidence is medium.
16. **Survey timing.** Fielded Sep 1–17, 2026: after the February sell-off, and before Salesforce agent metering and Workday's headless launch.

## 5. Dropped ideas (tested, did not hold)

- **Slide 4's "tepid renewals due to pricing pressure".** Soft-committed ratings are less price-led (9% vs. 15% name pricing their top renewal factor) and report the same renewal-hike pain (44% vs. 42%).
- **"Treat stated loyalty as an upper bound".** Filed retention is higher than stated intent.
- **"Disruption tracks AI maturity, not dissatisfaction".** Soft ratings are also more likely to plan a challenger path (55% vs. 41%), so both factors matter.
- **Any single switching cost predicting keep likelihood.** None does, except three of four in ITSM.
- **"Workflow disruption is the switching cost most exposed to AI".** Integration costs fall most. Data migration persists in pooled data and in CRM/ITSM, though it does fall in HRM/HCM.
- **Slide 3's planned 2x2 axes.** They are collinear (r = 0.93). The "Captive" quadrant is only directional.
- **HRM/HCM softness "holds within the same companies".** Mostly not, once HR buying experience is held constant; it persists only against ITSM.
- **"Lock-in has a price" as a new slide.** Only bundling and unused seats survive controls; renewal hikes and the AI premium don't. Demoted to a speaker note.
- **"Companies stay for automation and their own data" (slide 5).** An artifact of group size. "Few stay for the interface": the survey offered no UI option.
- **A paired CAN vs. CANNOT build barrier chart.** "Cannot" respondents saw an extra option that absorbed picks. Only time to market survives.
- **MIT NANDA's "internal builds succeed a third as often".** Methodology is described inconsistently between sources, and the finding is contested.
- **"1 in 5 cite security as both a reason to build and a barrier".** Barely above chance (21% vs. 19% expected).
- **Copilot citers (or citers of any feature) being more loyal.** Null for every driver. The CRM "unified data" hint (83% vs. 73%) was cherry-picked from 39 tests.
- **Company-level AI buying style predicting loyalty.** Null (72% vs. 70%).
- **"AI maturity leads to platform consolidation".** The opposite: maturity goes with hybrid.
- **Premium AI pricing pain leading to more incumbent-AI adoption (66% vs. 57%).** An artifact of how many areas each company answered; null at area level.
- **"Agent-first buyers resist usage pricing" under the exclusive definition.** Noise (25% vs. 18%, p = 0.11). It holds only for the broader group choosing agents/APIs.
- **"Remain as-is is most common in ERP/CRM because AI needs the cloud".** The area gaps are noise and the mechanism is untested.
- **"Middle Market is most aggressive on AI interfaces"** and **"Middle Market is stickiest on the system of record".** The first is a response-style artifact (p = 0.11); the second rests on CRM only.
- **96% as the headline stat, and "only 18% of chatbot choosers pick native".** Both are artifacts of the question format.
- **A tension between ITSM and Menlo's "incumbents hold IT".** Weak: ITSM replacement intent of 14% is within noise of HRM/HCM's 11%.
- **Q78 (cyber, n = 44), Q42 "No" (n = 44) and shadow IT among native-interface choosers.** Bases too small or hard to interpret.
- **Vertice "year on year" wording.** The source page doesn't say it.
